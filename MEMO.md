# Bending Spoons: what the numbers reveal

*Chrish Pep Felix A · from Bending Spoons' SEC filings up to Q2 2026 (US GAAP) · all figures in `output/`*

## Summary
Bending Spoons is a cash-generative software business whose headline growth is bought, not built: revenue rose 126% in
Q2 2026, but only 3% was organic. The acquired products convert profit into cash well and need almost no capital
spending, yet the deals are funded with debt, so net debt doubled to $4.1bn in six months and interest coverage fell
to 2.2x. The number I would watch most closely is organic growth, because it decides whether the debt can be paid down
if acquisitions pause.

## Five observations
1. **Growth is bought.** Q2 2026 revenue was $704m, up 126% year on year; organic growth was 3%, so acquisitions
   supplied about 123 of the 126 points (98% of the growth). Revenue rose from $601m in Q1 to $704m in Q2 as new
   businesses were consolidated.
2. **Adjusted profit flatters the picture.** The adjusted operating margin (54%) is 20 points above the US GAAP
   operating margin (34%). The two largest add-backs are amortisation of acquired intangibles (11.7% of revenue) and
   reorganisation costs (7.2%, about $51m). For a company whose model is to buy and restructure every quarter, I treat
   both as recurring costs of the strategy rather than one-offs.
3. **Strong cash conversion, but deals cost far more than the cash generated.** H1 2026 operating cash flow was $254m
   (1.24x net income) and capex only $4m, giving $250m of free cash flow (19% of revenue). That figure is already after
   $161m of interest paid, so interest absorbed about 39% of cash generated before interest. Acquisitions cost $2.29bn
   in the same period, 9x free cash flow, funded by $2.57bn of new debt.
4. **Leverage is rising quickly.** Net debt went from $2.04bn (December 2025) to $4.09bn (June 2026); debt to equity
   from 2.7x to 3.9x. Interest coverage (operating income / interest expense) fell from 2.8x to 2.2x year on year, and
   goodwill and intangibles are 78% of total assets.
5. **Guidance implies a steady Q4.** Q3 guidance of $733–745m (midpoint $739m, about 5% above Q2) and full-year guidance
   of $2.78–2.82bn imply Q4 revenue of about $756m, only 2% above Q3. That looks achievable on the current run-rate;
   the open question is whether it includes Airtable, which was announced after Q2.

## Risks
- **Interest costs.** If all $4.9bn of debt repriced 1.5–2 points higher, annual interest would rise by about
  $73–98m, roughly 15–20% of current annualised free cash flow. The real exposure depends on the fixed/floating mix.
- **Organic decline after restructuring.** If cost cuts and price increases at acquired products push organic growth
  below zero, revenue only grows through new debt-funded deals, and the 78% of assets that are goodwill and
  intangibles face impairment risk.

## Three questions I would ask the finance team
1. Of the 3% organic growth, how much comes from price increases and how much from customer or usage growth, and how
   does it differ between businesses owned for over two years and recent acquisitions?
2. How many quarters after an acquisition do reorganisation costs typically stop, and what share of them is cash?
3. What share of the debt is floating rate, and does the full-year guidance include Airtable?
